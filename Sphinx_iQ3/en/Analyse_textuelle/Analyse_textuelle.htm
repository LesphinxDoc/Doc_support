lexical workshop

The Lexical Workshop part allows you to process open questions and texts from external sources (internet, social networks, etc.). Sphinx iQ3's analysis methods and technologies allow you to perform different types of processing, through three protocols. All these protocols will help you to quickly identify themes and understand the meaning of the corpus studied.

  • Exploration Protocol 

    The Exploration protocol lets you examine the corpus in detail and "search the text" very quickly by taking advantage of all the resources of the lexical, semantic and statistical analysis.

  • Codification Protocol

    Allows automatic or manual construction of a thematic grid for encoding a corpus. The codification is used to classify observations based on their themes and to extract statistical data from them.

  • Synthesis Protocol

    Offers various syntheses to explain what the text is about (concepts), how we talk about it (positive / negative opinions), and what is the influence of a given context. These syntheses allow to quickly learn about the main themes and orientations of the corpus in a very short time and with little effort.

    Multilingual management: The lemmatization, orientation and sentiment functionalities can be used in English (NB: all observations must be in English).

GLOSSARY

Find here a glossary of terms used in the context of textual analysis.

General

  • The lemmatization is the process of reducing the different forms of the same word to their canonical form or lemma (singular for nouns and adjectives, infinitive for verbs). It is the basis of the textual analysis that leads to read a text from its lexicon. But a word can have several different meanings (polysemy) and the same meaning can correspond to different words (synonyms).

  • The semantic analysis helps overcome this problem by defining the necessary conditions to move from the lexicon to the sense (idea, concept, ...). It uses the thesaurus notions, ontology, or dictionary and semantic network.

  • The thesaurus defines a set of meanings, ideas / concepts and organizes them according to a tree nomenclature that goes from general to specific.

  • The ontology or a dictionary is a set of elements that define a notion. It is a set of words (in their lemmatised form) referring to a meaning and therefore a thesaurus entry.

  • The semantic network is a set of relationships between signifying elements (a word from a corpus or a meaning from a thesaurus) leading to clarify the meaning of these elements depending on the elements to which they are connected.

  • The sentiment is a sentence or a sentence segment characterized by: its type (Advice, judgment, feeling, opinion), its category (Recommendation, suggestion, evaluation, fear ...), its intensity (1 to 3), its orientation (positive, negative).

  • The context variable helps to understand the differences between respondents. It allows you to segment the corpus into several categories, such as age, gender, or department / service, etc. It must be a single closed variable or a closed scale variable.

  • The signature variable identifies the origin of the text. It highlights the characteristics of the person who is speaking. You can select multiple signature variables for the same analysis.

  • The length is the number of words in the response.

  • The richness is the number of different words in a response.

  • The banality is an average of words frequency in the corpus. The more frequent words are, the stronger the banality is.

ACCESS THE VARIOUS PROTOCOLS

To access the different textual and semantic analysis protocols, proceed as follows:

  1. From the Analysis of results section.

  2. Click on Lexical workshop, from the Home tab, Direct access section.

  3. Select the variable to analyze.

  4. Select a protocol according to the treatments you want to perform (Synthesis, Codification of content, Exploration).
