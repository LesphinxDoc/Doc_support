Recommendation Impact Score

Measure the impact of themes expressed in your verbatims on an overall evaluation. For each theme from a coding, view how its positive or negative mention influences the respondents' average score.

EXAMPLE: In a tourism survey, you can identify the themes from travelers' comments that have the greatest influence on their overall satisfaction, in order to distinguish key success factors from action priorities.

To create a RIS matrix, proceed as follows:

  1. Click the button.

  2. Select Textual and semantic analysis, then Recommendation Impact Score.

  3. Select the text variable to analyze.

    NOTE: The selected text variable must have been subject to sentence-level verbatim coding beforehand.

  4. Select the evaluation variable to correlate with the themes from the coding. This is usually the overall satisfaction variable.

    The themes are represented on a matrix according to two dimensions:

    • Vertical axis: impact

      Impact measures the influence of each theme on the overall evaluation. It is calculated from the correlation between the mention of the theme in the verbatims and the overall evaluation score. The higher the impact, the more the theme influences respondents' overall satisfaction.

    • Horizontal axis: orientation

      Orientation indicates whether a theme is mainly associated with positive or negative verbatims. Themes located on the right side of the matrix are mostly mentioned positively, while those located on the left side are more often associated with negative verbatims.

  5. (Optional) Modify the names of the axes and the different quadrants if needed.

  6. Click OK.

    After the matrix has been created, you can change the variables by selecting the analysis and clicking or by right-clicking the analysis and selecting Edit.

    • To improve the readability of the matrix, you can hide themes whose frequency is below a specified threshold. In the Calculation section, select Hide themes below the frequency, then specify the minimum frequency.

    • To improve the accuracy of topic orientation, select the Correct the orientations option, then select the evaluation variable to use.

      This variable takes the context of the response into account when detecting the orientation of verbatims.

      NOTE: Select a variable containing a satisfaction rating, such as a recommendation score ranging from 0 to 10.

PRESENTATION OPTIONS AND RIS MATRIX ANALYSIS

In the Options tab, under the Chart category, you can select Size proportional to counts. The size of the bubbles is then proportional to the counts of the variables studied.

The RIS matrix is composed of four areas:

  • Key success factors: The themes in this area have a positive impact on the overall evaluation and are mainly associated with positive verbatims. They represent the main satisfaction drivers and strengths to maintain.

  • Action priorities: The themes in this area have a strong impact on the overall evaluation, but are mainly associated with negative verbatims. They are priority areas for improvement because their perception strongly influences the overall score.

  • Secondary criteria: The themes in this area are mainly associated with negative verbatims, but their influence on the overall evaluation remains limited. They can be considered as part of an improvement process, without being a priority.

  • Secondary strengths: The themes in this area are associated with positive verbatims, but their impact on the overall evaluation is low. They highlight aspects appreciated by respondents, without being determining factors in their satisfaction.

As with other analyses, when many themes are displayed, the chart may become difficult to read. You can then zoom in on a specific area during preview to examine its themes more easily.

To zoom, select Allow zoom in runtime mode in the chart elements.

In the preview, place the cursor over part of the chart, then click and drag to draw a square around the area to zoom in on. You can then cancel the zoom by clicking the button.
