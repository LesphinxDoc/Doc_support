Glossary

Domain basics

Analysis section

Online or local statistical analysis and real-time data visualization (Datavisualization) software used to represent survey results interactively and according to a specific communication objective.

The online and local versions are distinct because some options are not available in both versions (for example, Continuous coding).

Population

Finite set of individuals or statistical units that you want to study.

“Individuals” are not necessarily people; they can be companies, hospitals, countries, etc.

The population of interest or target population is not necessarily the observed population.

  • Set of all units that meet a common definition and are the subject of the study. In statistics, the term refers to a finite or infinite set of elements from which observations are made, or to a set of individuals or elements with one or more characteristics to be examined.

Sample

Part or fraction of the target population defined according to a criterion such as gender, occupation, income level, place of residence, etc.

Unit used to assess other units of the same type.

Sphinx AI

Artificial intelligence available in Sphinx software. It lets you generate questionnaires in one click, quickly interpret results, and code verbatim responses in record time. Learn more about Sphinx AI.

Sphinx iQ

Desktop/local software for questionnaire design, management, and advanced data analysis. The current version is Sphinx iQ3.

SphinxOnline

Online software that complements iQ: survey distribution, response monitoring, data collection and updates, and online result sharing. Commonly abbreviated as SOL, with SOL 4 for version 4 and SOL 5 for version 5.

SPSS file

SPSS (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences) is software used for statistical analysis.

Study/Survey

A study of a question, issue, or topic carried out by collecting opinions, testimonials, and experiences (attitudes, behaviors, preferences) from a predefined group of individuals. Its main analysis tools are statistical in nature and it results in a written report.

The purpose of a survey result is to provide a “plausible answer” to a question or issue.

Qualitative study: carried out through successive interviews with several people or an interview with a single person.

Quantitative study:

  • Exhaustive study or census: carried out using a questionnaire involving a large number of people (all individuals in the population studied).

  • Survey-based study or sampling: carried out through a survey of a sample of people representative of the population studied.

Survey manager

Depending on their responsibilities, the survey manager is in charge of carrying out or monitoring a project in a specific professional environment, here statistical studies.

Triple-S file

The Survey Interchange Standard is a free, open standard used to transfer surveys, data, and metadata (variables, questions, response options) between survey software applications, regardless of hardware or software platform. It defines a way to transfer both data and survey variables between different survey programs running on separate hardware and software platforms. A survey is described using two files: a definition file (metadata), traditionally using the .sss extension, and an associated data file.

Study

Input quota

In relation to a target size, distribution of counts and expected number of responses according to the answer options of a question. “Enable a quota” means setting a limit on response entry. The quota method is a sampling method used to ensure that a sample is representative by assigning it a structure similar to that of the base population.

There are three possible cases when you use quotas:

  • QuotaComplete: The respondent belongs to one of the expected samples and the quota counts have not yet been reached. The respondent has the required profile, has completed the questionnaire, and their answers are saved.

  • QuotaFull: The target counts have already been reached. The respondent matches the required profile, but the expected number of responses has already been reached. Their answers are not useful, so they exit the survey and are told why they cannot respond.

  • ScreenOut: The respondent does not belong to any expected sample and does not have the required profile. Their answers are not useful, so they exit the survey and are told why they cannot respond.

Response/Answer

Complete submission of a questionnaire, finalized and saved entry. Respondent input for a question. A response can contain several response options.

Response input

Action or period during which the respondent enters their answers in the questionnaire.

Response monitoring

Monitoring of respondent participation status (collected response, drop-off in progress, immediate drop-off, etc.

Also called Response tracking.

Target count

Number of individuals in the target population. In this context, the number of responses expected to obtain a representative sample. See Response quota.

Questionnaire distribution and data collection

Automatic alert

The Automatic Alert feature allows you to monitor the evolution of an indicator without having to perform regular manual checks. It enables you to be automatically notified when a key indicator shows a significant variation, upward or downward, between two defined analysis periods. This rapid detection makes it easier to identify any trend breaks or anomalies, helping you to react more quickly.

Automatic sending regulation

Voluntary or automatic slowing of the sending rate for an email send with a large recipient list. The send is spread out according to values defined beforehand.

Campaign

Structured set of sends or distributions related to the same questionnaire. A distribution campaign includes all actions used to send a questionnaire to one or more groups of recipients, through one or more channels (email, link, SMS, etc.) according to a defined sending strategy. See Distribution channel.

Data collection

Data collection is the systematic collection and measurement of information from different sources, which is then used for decision-making, insight generation, and feeding data-driven systems.

Data collection is the first step in the data life cycle. It represents all raw information collected for an organization before it is processed, stored, and analyzed.

Deliverability

Set of parameters that affect whether an email is properly delivered to its recipient. Ability of an email to reach recipients’ inbox.

Distribution channel

Method used to distribute the questionnaire to respondents.

At Le Sphinx: Email/SMS, direct link, QR code, Web integration (iframe and pop-in), as well as SphinxSurvey, Taqt devices, WebReporting/Reporting (for entered actions), etc.

Emailing

Sending bulk emails to a list of recipients from a database. Often used by businesses to promote their offers and services.

External sending

Method for sending an invitation or reminder email using an emailing service other than Sphinx solutions. Also called an external send.

Merge field

Tag or marker that acts as a placeholder and is populated by the software using information from a database.

EXAMPLE: When writing an email, entering “Dear [Name]” displays “Dear John...”.

Multichannel distribution

If you create several distribution campaigns using different channels for the same survey, your survey uses multichannel distribution.

Over-solicitation

Excessive sending of messages to the same recipient over a limited period. It can lead to recipient fatigue, lower engagement, more unsubscribes, or spam reports.

With Sphinx solutions, you can limit the number of solicitations (sends in general and/or automated reminders) sent to the same recipient to prevent over-solicitation.

Over-solicited

Depending on the over-solicitation criteria defined by the user, an over-solicited recipient has been contacted too often or has too low an engagement rate and cannot be contacted.

Recipient

Person targeted by the distribution campaign to answer the questionnaire. May correspond to the respondent. See Campaign.

Reminder

Message sent after an initial invitation, taking into account the status of the recipients of that initial invitation. See Send.

Not to be confused with Invitation.

Return rate

Percentage of people who answered a survey compared with the total number of people targeted in the sample.

Self-service

Providing respondents with the device used to enter responses (tablet, smartphone, etc.).

Send/sending

Action of sending an invitation or reminder by email or SMS.

Sending statistics

Page that summarizes the number of recipients in a campaign, their status, and their response entry status.

Analysis Section

Adjustment

In marketing research, sample adjustment aims to improve the representativeness of the surveyed sample according to qualification criteria. The underlying principle is that only a sample with the same structure as the parent population for known criteria can be used to generalize answers obtained on other criteria to the entire population. Adjustment therefore applies weights to individuals to increase the weight of underrepresented groups in the surveyed sample compared with the parent population, and to reduce the weight of overrepresented groups.

Analysis view

Data visualization medium.

Generic term that includes, among other things, infographics, dashboards, and reports. Used in Sphinx solutions to refer to any representation of results created with the Analysis feature. It is therefore a view of data analysis.

Automatic analysis

Inventory of survey results before analysis.

Configuration

WebReporting is divided into several configurations. Each configuration is linked to the same Reporting and therefore to the same survey.

A configuration is a “point of view”: each configuration has its own defined actions. You can share a different configuration with each person, give a person access to several configurations, etc.

Cross sort

A cross-tabulation results from crossing the results obtained for two questions (or variables) in a questionnaire.

Cross-tabulations highlight behavioral differences among the subpopulations studied or the existence of explanatory variables and correlations between two variables.

In a survey about smoking, it may be useful to cross-tabulate smoker/non-smoker status with age or gender.

Dashboard

Data visualization medium highlighting the main performance indicators of a company on a single page.

Size: 1400 x 1500 px (W x H)

Usually used to monitor real-time indicators and provides an interactive, filterable dashboard (period, profiles, etc.)

Tool for evaluating the organization of a company or institution (business intelligence). It makes it possible to monitor changes in results and gaps compared with reference values (objectives, standards, statistical references).

A dashboard is a web-dedicated format that centralizes essential information on a single page to provide a quick view of results. It therefore has a larger format than a report because it is not intended for printing.

Data visualization

Set of methods used to summarize qualitative and quantitative statistical data graphically in order to highlight relationships between data.

Visual representations of raw data used to format it in a readable and understandable way.

Also called Dataviz, Datavisualization, or graphical representation of data.

Dispersion

Statistical dispersion is the tendency of the values in the distribution of a characteristic to spread out around a central value and/or move away from one another. This calculation is meaningful only for quantitative characteristics.

A distinction is made between absolute dispersion parameters (measured in the unit of measurement of the characteristic) and relative dispersion parameters (measured by a dimensionless number).

Flat sort

Statistical method used to analyze data by counting the number of occurrences for each response option or answer option of a question.

Provides the distribution of answers question by question: it is the first statistical processing performed.

Also called univariate analysis.

Infographic

Data visualization medium in a concise and organized format.

Size: 600 x 2000 px (W x H)

Aims to summarize and contextualize information that is difficult to explain using words alone. Used in marketing and communication strategy.

Simplified representation of results used to:

  • help users understand at a glance;

  • popularize and make complex information accessible;

  • highlight important information;

  • summarize a report.

Llosa matrix

The Llosa matrix consists of classifying factors likely to affect the satisfaction-dissatisfaction relationship of consumers with a service in general, based on two criteria: the performance level of the factors and their influence on overall satisfaction.

This relationship produces a map made up of four quadrants:

  • Plus elements (excellence factors): factors that contribute strongly to satisfaction when perceived positively and weakly to dissatisfaction when perceived negatively.

  • Key elements (driving factors): criteria that have a strong influence on satisfaction when perceived positively and on dissatisfaction when perceived negatively.

  • Basic elements (essential factors): criteria that contribute strongly to dissatisfaction when perceived negatively and weakly to satisfaction when perceived positively.

  • Secondary elements (neutral factors): factors that have little influence on satisfaction, regardless of perception.

Mean

The mean is the simplest indicator used to summarize the information provided by a set of statistical data: it is equal to the sum of the data divided by the number of data points. It can therefore be calculated using only these two elements, without knowing the entire distribution.

The mean of a distribution is not always the best indicator: the median is often more relevant. However, calculating it requires knowing the entire distribution, or at least its central part.

Also called Average.

Median

When a distribution is ordered, the median divides it into two parts with equal counts.

For a salary distribution, 50% of salaries are below the median and 50% are above it.

Many economic quantities have a lower limit but no upper limit. For example, hourly wages are limited at the lower end by the minimum wage, while some salaries can be very high. The mean is pulled upward by high salaries, even if they are few in number, and is therefore generally higher than the median.

NPS

The Net Promoter Score is a measure used to assess customer satisfaction and their likelihood of recommending a company, product, or service to others. Respondents are then grouped into three categories: detractors (0 to 6), passives (7 to 8), and promoters (9 to 10).

The NPS score is calculated by subtracting the percentage of detractors from the percentage of promoters.

Profile

Set of characteristics that define a category of people or users.

Defined using a profile variable.

Profile variable

Variable whose response options define the characteristics of a profile.

Qualitative study

Qualitative studies include interviewing and observation methodologies. They traditionally rely on individual interviews and group discussions with limited samples, ranging from a few units to a few dozen people.

They include in-situation observation and document collection. They aim to understand a market and interpret a material, social, or symbolic reality. They draw on the humanities and social sciences (ethnology, psychology, sociology, semiology, linguistics) and general knowledge to make sense of consumer experiences, customer feedback, or social phenomena. The focus is on collecting verbal data rather than numerical data.

Quantitative study

Quantitative studies, often contrasted with qualitative studies, are associated with measurement. Methods include panels, barometers, and surveys conducted with samples of several hundred or even several thousand individuals. Their purpose is to generalize results across a large number of people. A quantitative study is used to validate trends, measure demand for a product, assess interest in a service, or describe a population.

Report

Data visualization and results interpretation medium.

Size: 16:9 screen or A4 format

The report presents figures and interprets them. It contains several pages and is therefore more written than a dashboard or infographic. It is used to evaluate the organization or performance of a company or institution (business intelligence).

In A4 format, it is intended for printing.

A report is a more detailed and complete format that can be intended for a future PowerPoint format or for printing. It must therefore use the standard 16:9 format. Unlike a dashboard, a report consists of several pages.

Sample

Element of a sample, subset, or subpopulation of a group studied that is homogeneous, meaning it includes all individuals in a population with common characteristics.

Significance threshold

Threshold from which test results are considered reliable. In other words, this threshold determines confidence in the correlation between a test performed and the results obtained.

Standard deviation

Standard deviation measures the dispersion, or spread, of a set of values around their mean. The lower the standard deviation, the more homogeneous the population.

WebReporting

Customizable online platform that centralizes all study results. It is a complete solution for easily sharing results and monitoring them in real time. Learn more about WebReporting.

Textual analysis

Banality

Average frequency of words in the corpus. The more frequent the words, the higher the commonness.

Codebook

Coding grid used to structure and analyze qualitative data, that is, respondents’ text answers (comments, customer reviews, etc.). Its main role is to guide research managers when assigning categories to received comments.

Concept

General idea or abstract representation of an object or set of objects with common characteristics.

Continuous Codification – by AI

Available only in the online Analysis section.

Coding performed by artificial intelligence for each new verbatim response saved, according to the predefined Codebook.

Corpus

A corpus is a set of text data or documents collected and analyzed to answer specific research questions. A corpus can include various types of texts, such as interview transcripts, answers to open-ended questionnaire questions, press articles, emails, social media posts, reports, etc.

Dictionary-based thematic analysis

The purpose of thematic analysis is to distribute, classify, and organize the vocabulary used by respondents into categories, which are themselves divided into themes.

The set produced by distributing vocabulary into these categories and themes is called a dictionary.

Each dictionary has its own thematic organization (hierarchy or organization of categories or themes).

Lemmatization

Lemmatization is the process of reducing the different forms of the same word to its canonical form or lemma (singular masculine form for nouns and adjectives; infinitive for verbs). It is the basis of lexical analysis, which helps understand a text through its lexicon. However, a word can have several different meanings (polysemy), and the same meaning can correspond to different words (synonymy).

Lexical analysis

Lexical analysis is the study of the vocabulary used in data collected during a survey or interviews. It focuses on words: their richness, frequency, redundancy, absence, etc. Lexical analysis makes it possible to quickly understand how a target audience expresses itself and therefore to better adapt communication to that audience.

Orientation

Character of a verbatim response based on the evaluation left by the respondent; interpretation of the respondent’s opinion or assessment. A verbatim response can be: Positive, Mixed, Negative, or No opinion.

Orientation is determined for the entire observation/verbatim response and may differ by sentence or syntagm.

EXAMPLE: Annecy is a beautiful city! The lake is breathtaking. Only downside: traffic...

Semantics

Semantics is the study of the meaning of words.

Semantic analysis

Semantic analysis complements lexical analysis. It focuses on the meaning of words in a sentence and in the context of that sentence to identify the meaning of verbatim responses.

Textual analysis

Textual analysis transforms an unstructured database (survey verbatim responses, interview transcripts, articles, etc.) into measurable structured data (words, themes, sentiments, etc.) to reveal the meaning of the text. Textual analysis therefore helps understand how people communicate their opinions.

Theme/Orientation Cross-analysis

Available only in the online Analysis section.

Analysis that combines dictionary-based thematic analysis and verbatim orientation to compare or relate them.

You can also perform a coding/orientation cross-analysis, which combines coding and verbatim orientation to compare or relate them.

Thesaurus

Organized list of units specific to a field that presents structured semantic relationships and contains terminological rules used to translate concepts expressed in natural language into an artificial language.

At Le Sphinx, the thesaurus is a list that groups a set of predefined concepts and acts as a semantic engine for text analyses.

The Thesaurus is divided into four main categories:

  • General themes

  • Main ideas

  • Detailed concepts

  • Very detailed concepts

The user can therefore choose the level of detail for their analyses.

Verbatim

A verbatim is the literal transcription of answers provided by participants in a survey, interview, or focus group. It is the faithful reproduction or restitution of participants’ answers exactly as they were expressed, without modification or interpretation.

Verbatim codification

The purpose of codification is to distribute observations or sentences from the corpus into categories, which are themselves divided into themes.

The set produced by distributing observations and sentences into themes is called a Codebook.

The Codebook is unique: a single codification variable is created.

Codification can be manual or automated using artificial intelligence:

  • Coding an observation based on the Codebook created by the user: AI associates the verbatim response with the corresponding themes;

  • Coding a set of observations based on the Codebook created by the user: AI associates the verbatim responses with the corresponding themes;

  • Complete creation of the Codebook (requires specifying a context and expected format);

  • Continuous codification.

Word Cloud

Visualization method that displays how often words appear in a given body of text by making the size of each word proportional to its frequency. The words are then arranged in a group or word cloud.
